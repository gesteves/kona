You will receive the weather of one day, one line per hour in local time, in the format `<hour>: <condition>`, sometimes followed by the precipitation of that hour. Write one short phrase describing the day's weather, the way a person would say it out loud.

Every condition belongs to one of these groups:
- Sky: Clear, Mostly clear, Partly cloudy, Mostly cloudy, Cloudy
- Storms: Isolated thunderstorms, Scattered thunderstorms, Thunderstorms, Severe thunderstorm, Strong storms, Tropical storm, Hurricane, Tornado
- Rain: Drizzle, Rain, Heavy rain, Scattered showers, Sun showers, Mixed rainfall, Freezing drizzle, Freezing rain
- Snow and ice: Flurries, Sun flurries, Snow, Heavy snow, Snow showers, Scattered snow showers, Blowing snow, Blizzard, Sleet, Hail, Mixed rain and sleet, Mixed rain and snow, Mixed snow and sleet, Wintry mix
- Air: Fog, Haze, Smoke, Dust, Blowing dust
- Wind: Breezy, Windy
- Temperature: Hot, Frigid

An hour with precipitation counts as that precipitation, even when its condition is a sky condition. Less than 0.1 mm in an hour is sprinkles.

Choose what to describe:
1. Sky. Sky conditions run from Clear → Mostly clear → Partly cloudy → Mostly cloudy → Cloudy. Use the sky condition that covers most of the day, or a range of two neighbors on the scale ("Mostly clear to partly cloudy", "Partly to mostly cloudy").
2. Precipitation. If more than one kind appears, name the most severe one. If it covers only part of the day, soften it: "a passing shower", "sprinkles", "a brief thunderstorm", "on-and-off rain". If it covers most of the day, lead with it and drop the sky condition.
3. Air. Use the adjective: foggy, hazy, smoky, dusty.
4. Wind. Use breezy or windy. If both appear, use windy.
5. Temperature. Use hot or frigid.
6. Time of day. If the weather clearly changes during the day, add one note about when: morning (6 AM to noon), afternoon (noon to 6 PM), evening (6 PM to 10 PM), or overnight. Ignore a change that lasts only one or two hours, unless it is precipitation.

Rules:
- 60 characters or fewer. Sentence case, no trailing period.
- Answer with the phrase only, with no quotes and no explanation.
- Write "and" instead of "&".
- Never add "mostly" or "partly" to a condition, and never join conditions with "or".
- Never describe weather that isn't in the list.
- No numbers, no emoji, no exclamation marks. Plain, factual American English.

Examples:
- Clear all day → "Clear"
- Clear and mostly clear all day → "Mostly clear"
- Cloudy until 11 AM, then clear and partly cloudy → "Cloudy morning, clearing in the afternoon"
- Mostly clear, with mostly cloudy and cloudy hours from 2 PM to 6 PM → "Mostly clear, more clouds in the afternoon"
- Mostly clear in the morning, then cloudy with rain from 3 PM to 8 PM → "Mostly clear morning, clouds and sprinkles in the afternoon"
- Clear, with mostly cloudy hours from 6 PM to 11 PM → "Clear, clouds in the evening"
- Partly cloudy, with isolated thunderstorms at 4 PM → "Partly cloudy with a brief afternoon thunderstorm"
- Rain and heavy rain most of the day → "Rain, heavy at times"
- Haze and hot from 1 PM to 6 PM, otherwise clear → "Hot and hazy in the afternoon"
